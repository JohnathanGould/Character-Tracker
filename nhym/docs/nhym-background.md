# Nhym Ashcloak: background

## Where his story began

Nhym grew up in the middle of Shadowdale, not under it. His father, Boorga, has served as manservant in Jamble the Eye's house for as long as Nhym can remember, and the Ashcloak family lived under that roof alongside Jamble's wife Leel and their son Serbon. It was a crowded house and a busy one. Jamble ran his trade in odd and interesting goods out of it, buyers and sellers from Suzail to Hillsfar passing through at all hours, and Nhym spent his childhood underfoot in the middle of it, learning to look closely at what people carried.

Boorga's own family carries old blood. His people are an offshoot of the deep gnomes, the svirfneblin, a small enclave that broke from the greater Underdark communities generations ago and burrowed even deeper, into root-caverns beneath Cormanthor known to a handful of Harper cartographers as the Whisperwarrens. Where most svirfneblin build fortress-holds to withstand the Underdark's horrors, Boorga's clan chose the opposite path: they made themselves impossible to notice. No walls, no banners, just tunnels that breathed like lungs, sealed and reopened by gnomes who moved without sound. His kin call themselves the Quiet Root. Outsiders who've heard the stories, usually secondhand and usually disbelieved, call them Whisper Gnomes.

Boorga left that world behind before Nhym was born, trading the Whisperwarrens for a manservant's post in a surface household, and the old stories had mostly worn down to something told over ale by the time Nhym was a boy, half legend, half embarrassment, not quite believed even by the man telling them.

## Apprenticeship

Jamble likes to say he is a former criminal who now steals legally, and most of Shadowdale watches its purses around him anyway. Nhym grew up close enough to that reputation to learn from it directly, but not the part most people assume. Jamble's real gift, and the one he actually bothered to teach, was knowing what a thing was worth before anyone else in the room did. Nhym spent years at his side learning to read goods the way other apprentices read ledgers: a chip in the glaze that meant a vase was older than its seller claimed, a weight in a coin purse that didn't match its coins, a blade's temper line that told you who really forged it.

He never had much patience for the other side of the trade, the contacts, the favors owed, the rumors bought over ale in the back rooms of the Old Skull Inn. That was Jamble's own skillset, honed over decades, and Nhym always felt like a poor student of it, more interested in the object in front of him than the network of people talking about it. What people call his instincts for trouble came from somewhere plainer: paying close attention. He learned early to watch a room, listen past the noise of it, and notice what didn't belong, because that was the same skill Jamble used to spot a forged provenance across a crowded stall. It just turned out to work as well on people as it did on goods.

Jamble never called any of it thieving. He called it "understanding value from every angle." Nhym learned the trade well enough that he can no longer tell where Jamble's honest business ends and the other kind begins, and he has stopped trying to draw the line.

## Family

Boorga's household inside Jamble's house was its own small world: a wife, a daughter, and Nhym himself, all raised alongside Jamble's own family. Serbon, Jamble's son, grew up more like a brother than an employer's child, close enough in age and mischief that the two of them account for a fair share of Shadowdale's minor unsolved disappearances of small valuable objects over the years, most of which were eventually returned, quietly, before anyone official got involved.

Whatever old blood runs in Boorga's line, it never sat well with Nhym as a boy. He grew up wanting to belong to the town in front of him, not to a story about somewhere else. Sunlight, crowds, the noise of a working household, all of it felt like his, in a way the family stories never quite did.

## Faith

Nhym isn't devout in any way the Dale's temple-goers would recognize. He doesn't pray on schedule, doesn't tithe, and would look genuinely confused if you called him pious. What he has is a habit, picked up sometime in his teens and never quite shaken: a coin flipped before anything risky, a murmured word to Tymora before a lock, a ledge, or a lie. Lady Luck fit him before he ever thought hard about why. Jamble's trade ran on knowing the odds on a piece of goods before you paid for it, and Nhym applied the same instinct to his own life, that everything worth doing came down to a fair risk, well judged, and a little grace when the judging wasn't enough.

It suits a boy raised half in Boorga's world and half in Jamble's, belonging fully to neither. The Quiet Root has its own quiet gods, old and careful ones that favor patience over chance, and Nhym never felt at home with them any more than he did with the family stories themselves. Tymora asks nothing of him but nerve, and nerve is the one thing growing up in that house taught him plenty of. He carries a battered silver coin, worn smooth on one face, and flips it before anything that matters. He'd never call it a prayer. He'd call it checking the odds.
